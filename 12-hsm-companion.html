Sahaj Pranayama — Natural Breath Awareness
5–10 min · Beginner-friendly · Any time
›
The foundational HSM breath practice. Simply observing the natural breath without controlling it — bringing awareness to the sensation of air entering and leaving the nostrils. Calms the fluctuating mind and develops sustained attention.
1
Sit comfortably — on a chair or floor. Spine naturally upright. Hands resting in the lap.
2
Close your eyes gently. Take one slow, deliberate breath to settle.
3
Bring your attention to the nostrils. Simply observe the cool sensation of air entering, the brief pause, and the warm sensation as it leaves.
4
When the mind wanders — which is natural — gently notice this and return to the breath sensation. No judgment. Every return is a success.
5
Continue for 5–10 minutes. End by sitting quietly for one minute before opening your eyes.

So-Hum Mantra with Breath
10–20 min · Intermediate · Morning / evening
›
So-Hum (Sanskrit: "I am That") is a natural sound of the breath — "So" on inhalation, "Hum" on exhalation. Syncing this ancient mantra with the breath creates a bridge between conscious attention and deeper awareness. Used in HSM for de-identification with mental noise.
1
Settle into a comfortable seated position. Close your eyes and take three slow breaths to arrive.
2
Begin to hear "So" silently as you inhale. Feel the chest and abdomen expand.
3
Hear "Hum" silently as you exhale. Feel the body soften and release.
4
Let the breath be natural — don't force its pace. The mantra follows the breath, not the reverse.
5
When thoughts arise, neither push them away nor follow them. Let them pass like clouds. Return to So-Hum.
6
After 10–20 minutes, let the mantra dissolve. Rest in silence for 2 minutes before opening your eyes.

Pratyahara Body Scan
10–15 min · All levels · Evening / pre-sleep
›
Pratyahara means "withdrawal of the senses" — systematically releasing physical tension by bringing and then withdrawing attention from each part of the body. Highly effective for somatisation, chronic pain, sleep disorders, and post-trauma grounding.
1
Lie down comfortably. Cover yourself with a light blanket if needed. Allow the body to be completely still.
2
Begin with three deep breaths. Let the body sink into the surface beneath you.
3
Bring attention to the top of your head. Just feel whatever is there — warmth, tingling, nothing. Then release that area.
4
Slowly move down: forehead, eyes, jaw (where we hold so much tension), neck, shoulders, chest, abdomen, hands, lower back, hips, thighs, knees, calves, feet.
5
At each area: notice → breathe → release. No judgment about what you find.
6
At the end, rest in whole-body awareness for 2–5 minutes. Drift into sleep or slowly return to wakefulness.

Metta — Loving Kindness Radiation
10–15 min · All levels · Morning / anytime
›
Metta (loving-kindness) is cultivated in concentric circles — beginning with oneself, expanding to loved ones, neutral people, difficult people, and finally all beings. Core to HSM's non-dual teachings. Particularly powerful for guilt, grief, interpersonal conflict, and depression.
1
Sit comfortably. Bring one hand gently to the heart. Take three slow breaths.
2
To yourself: Silently repeat — "May I be well. May I be safe. May I be at peace. May I be happy." Feel each phrase rather than just thinking it.
3
To a loved one: Picture someone you love. Send the same wishes: "May you be well. May you be safe. May you be at peace. May you be happy."
4
To a neutral person: Someone you see regularly but have no strong feeling for. Same phrases.
5
To a difficult person: Someone who challenges you. Extend the same wishes — not because they deserve it, but because your heart benefits from the expansion.
6
To all beings: Expand awareness to your city, your country, the world. "May all beings be well, safe, peaceful, and happy."

HSM practices combine ancient Himalayan wisdom with modern understanding of the autonomic nervous system. Here is what the evidence and tradition tell us.
🧠
Neurological
Sustained meditation practice increases prefrontal cortical thickness, reduces amygdala reactivity, and improves attentional networks (Davidson et al.; Lazar et al.).
❤️
Cardiovascular
Slow coherent breathing (5–6/min) maximises heart rate variability — a strong marker of cardiovascular health and stress resilience.
😴
Sleep
Body scan and 4-7-8 breathing activate the parasympathetic system, reduce cortisol, and facilitate sleep onset. CBT-I compatible.
🔥
Stress & burnout
Regular practice lowers perceived stress scores (PSS) and salivary cortisol within 4–8 weeks. Strong evidence in healthcare worker burnout populations.
🤝
Relationships
Metta (loving-kindness) practice reduces interpersonal conflict, increases prosocial behaviour, and improves self-compassion — a mediator in depression treatment.
🧘
De-addiction support
Urge surfing through breath awareness reduces craving intensity and frequency. Mindfulness-based relapse prevention (MBRP) has strong evidence in SUD.
🌱
Spiritual growth
HSM teachings emphasise that mental health is inseparable from the inner life. Practices support a gradual shift from reactivity to witnessing awareness.
👶
For all ages
2-2 breathing and natural breath awareness are safe and beneficial from childhood. Adapted versions are used in child/adolescent mental health and geriatric care.
