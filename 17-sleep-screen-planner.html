CBT-I — the evidence base
Cognitive Behavioural Therapy for Insomnia · First-line treatment
What is CBT-I?
CBT-I is recommended as the first-line treatment for chronic insomnia by the American College of Physicians, NICE (UK), and the European Sleep Research Society — preferred over sleep medication. It typically consists of 4–8 sessions and produces durable improvements that outlast pharmacological treatment.
Sleep restriction therapy
Temporarily limit time in bed to actual sleep time, building homeostatic sleep pressure. Counterintuitive but highly effective — produces deep, consolidated sleep.
Stimulus control
Use the bed only for sleep and intimacy. If not asleep within 20 min, get up. Retrains the brain to associate bed with sleepiness, not wakefulness.
Sleep hygiene education
Consistent wake time, limiting caffeine and alcohol, managing light exposure, temperature, exercise timing. Necessary but insufficient alone.
Cognitive restructuring
Addressing catastrophic thoughts about sleep loss ("I must sleep 8 hours", "Tomorrow will be ruined"). Hyperarousal driven by dysfunctional sleep beliefs is a primary maintaining factor.
Relaxation training
Progressive muscle relaxation, diaphragmatic breathing, guided imagery. Targets physiological hyperarousal that prevents sleep onset and maintenance.
Blue light & circadian biology
Short-wavelength (blue) light from screens suppresses melatonin secretion by up to 3 hours and delays circadian phase. Dim, warm-tone lighting 2h before bed is biologically significant.
Pharmacotherapy note: Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs (zolpidem, zopiclone) are effective short-term but do not address perpetuating factors and carry dependence risk. Melatonin is appropriate for circadian phase disorders and jet lag. CBT-I addresses the underlying mechanisms. The two are not mutually exclusive — short-course medication can bridge acute distress while CBT-I builds durable skills.
