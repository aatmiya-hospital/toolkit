Clinical use guide
How to use cognitive restructuring in session
When to use
Depression (especially negative cognitive triad — self, world, future), anxiety (threat appraisal), OCD (overvalued beliefs), PTSD (guilt, shame beliefs), adjustment disorders, caregiver burnout. Less appropriate for acute psychosis — address distortions after stabilisation and with lower challenge intensity.
Session sequence
1. Identify a specific, recent triggering situation
2. Elicit the automatic thought — not the emotion, the thought
3. Rate belief in the thought (0–100%)
4. Name the emotion and rate intensity
5. Identify the distortion type
6. Gather evidence for and against
7. Construct balanced thought
8. Re-rate belief and emotions
9. Assign homework — self-monitoring form between sessions
Indian context adaptations
• Collectivist framing: "What would your family think if they knew this thought?" can elicit external perspectives
• Karma and fate beliefs may interact with personalisation and fortune-telling — work respectfully, not against these beliefs
• Self-criticism (mental filter, personalisation) is very common in presentations shaped by shame culture
• Should statements often reflect family/social expectations — explore whose voice the "should" belongs to
• For low-literacy patients: use the distortion library verbally rather than as a reading task
Common pitfalls
• Disputing too aggressively — the goal is examination, not persuasion
• Skipping the evidence-gathering step — balanced thoughts without evidence are just positive thinking
• Challenging beliefs that are realistic (sometimes people really are being discriminated against, or the situation is genuinely dire — validate first)
• Using cognitive techniques for profound grief or trauma loss before emotional validation is complete
