Key principle
Assertiveness is not aggression. It is the ability to express your feelings, needs, and rights clearly and directly — while respecting the other person's feelings, needs, and rights. The goal is mutual understanding, not winning.
I-Statement builder
Build an assertive response step by step
The I-Statement formula: "When [behaviour], I feel [emotion], because [impact]. I would like [specific request]."
It focuses on behaviour (not personality), takes ownership of feelings (not blame), and makes a clear request.
Step 1 — The behaviour
"When you…" — describe the specific, observable behaviour. No interpretations, no character judgments.
Step 2 — Your feeling
"I feel…" — one emotion word. Not "I feel that you…" (which is a thought, not a feeling).
Worried
Hurt
Frustrated
Disrespected
Anxious
Overwhelmed
Unheard
Sad
Angry
Unsupported
Step 3 — The impact / because
"Because…" — why does this behaviour affect you? What does it mean or lead to?
Step 4 — The request
"I would like…" — specific, realistic, positive request. What do you want them to DO (not stop doing)?

Clinical applications
How assertiveness training fits psychiatric and corporate settings
Depression
Passive communication style is strongly associated with depression — not expressing needs leads to unmet needs, resentment, and deepening low mood. Behavioural activation + assertiveness training is a core CBT skill cluster for depression.
Anxiety & social phobia
Fear of conflict and negative evaluation drives passive / passive-aggressive patterns. Graded assertiveness practice — starting with low-stakes situations — is a key exposure component in CBT for social anxiety and generalised anxiety disorder.
De-addiction / SUD
Inability to assertively refuse substance offers from peers / family is a major relapse trigger. Drink / drug refusal skills training is a form of assertiveness training — practising specific refusal scripts with role-play. Included in MBRP, CBT-SUD, and social skills training protocols.
Indian family context
In joint families, assertiveness must be balanced with collectivist cultural values. Frame it as "honest, respectful communication that strengthens relationships" rather than "standing up for yourself" — the latter can sound selfish in Indian cultural context. Adapt scenarios to reflect Indian family and workplace dynamics.
Corporate wellness sessions
High-value skill for group workshops — especially for teacher populations (boundary-setting with school management), IT workers (workload negotiation), and healthcare workers (expressing concerns to seniors). The 4-styles comparison and I-statement builder work well as group exercises.
Session structure for a 60-minute assertiveness workshop
0–10 min Psychoeducation: What is assertiveness? Why does it matter?
10–20 min 4-styles exercise: identify your default style
20–35 min I-statement building — individual practice with shared scenarios
35–50 min Role-play in pairs: one person plays the difficult situation, one practices assertive response
50–60 min Debrief: what felt hard? What surprised you? One thing to practice this week.
