Why family involvement matters
Family support is one of the strongest predictors of recovery in psychiatric illness.
In India, the joint family is both a resource and a source of stress — understanding both is essential.
Informed families reduce relapse rates, improve medication adherence, and lower expressed emotion (EE).
Caregivers also need support — burnout in caregivers directly worsens patient outcomes.
This programme covers six key areas that every family member of someone with a mental illness should understand. You do not need any medical background — just patience and willingness to learn. Each section can be read in 5–10 minutes and is available in both English and Gujarati.
આ કાર્યક્રમ છ મુખ્ય વિષયો આવરે છે જે માનસિક બીમારીથી પીડાતા વ્યક્તિના દરેક પરિવારના સભ્યે સમજવા જોઈએ. તમારે કોઈ તબીબી જ્ઞાનની જરૂર નથી — ફક્ત ધૈર્ય અને શીખવાની ઇચ્છા. દરેક ભાગ ૫–૧૦ મિનિટમાં વાંચી શકાય છે.

Understanding mental illness
માનસિક બીમારી સમજો
Mental illness is a medical condition — not a personal failure, spiritual failing, or weakness of character. The brain is an organ, just like the heart or kidney. When the brain's chemistry or function is disrupted, the result is a psychiatric illness. It is no different from diabetes or hypertension in this regard.
માનસિક બીમારી એ એક તબીબી સ્થિતિ છે — કોઈ વ્યક્તિગત નિષ્ફળતા, આધ્યાત્મિક ખામી અથવા ચારિત્ર્યની નબળાઈ નથી. મગજ એ હૃદય અથવા કિડની જેવું જ એક અંગ છે. જ્યારે મગજની રાસાયણિક ક્રિયા ખોરવાય છે, ત્યારે માનસિક બીમારી ઉત્પન્ન થાય છે. આ ડાયાબિટીસ કે હાઈ બ્લડ પ્રેશર જેવી જ છે.
What the family should know
Recovery is possible. Most people improve significantly with treatment.
Medication often needs weeks to work — stopping early is a common cause of relapse.
The person is not "mad" — they are unwell. Use respectful language.
Treatment is a combination of medicine, therapy, family support, and lifestyle.
Depression is not laziness, sadness, or "just tension." It is a real illness where the brain's mood-regulating systems are disrupted. The person experiences persistent low mood, loss of interest, fatigue, poor sleep, changes in appetite, and sometimes thoughts of death. In the Indian context, depression often presents as physical complaints — headache, bodyache, tiredness — rather than "feeling sad."
ડિપ્રેશન આળસ, ઉદાસી, અથવા "ફક્ત ટેન્શન" નથી. તે એક વાસ્તવિક બીમારી છે. ભારતીય સંદર્ભમાં, ડિપ્રેશન ઘણી વખત શારીરિક ફરિયાદ — માથાનો દુખાવો, શરીરનો દુખાવો, થાક — તરીકે આવે છે.
✓ Helpful
✓ Sit with them without pressure to talk
✓ Accompany them to appointments
✓ Ensure medication is taken regularly
✓ Gently encourage small activities
✗ Harmful
✗ "Cheer up / think positive"
✗ "You have everything, why sad?"
✗ Pressuring to perform duties
✗ Dismissing as laziness
Anxiety disorders involve excessive, uncontrollable worry, physical symptoms (racing heart, breathlessness, sweating, chest tightness), and avoidance. In India, anxiety is often expressed through somatic complaints and may be culturally framed as "weakness" or "overthinking." Panic attacks are frequently misdiagnosed as heart attacks and can be frightening for both patient and family.
ચિંતા વિકારમાં અતિશય, અનિયંત્રણ ચિંતા અને શારીરિક લક્ષણો (ઝડપી ધબકારા, શ્વાસ ચઢવો, પરસેવો) સામેલ છે. ભારતમાં, ચિંતા ઘણી વખત "નબળાઈ" અથવા "વધુ પડતી ચિંતા" તરીકે ઓળખાવાય છે.
During a panic attack — family response
Stay calm. Your calm directly helps them co-regulate.
Speak slowly and softly: "You are safe. This will pass."
Guide them to breathe with you — slow, deliberate breaths.
Do NOT rush to the hospital unless there are actual chest symptoms warranting it — this reinforces the panic pattern.
Psychosis (including schizophrenia, bipolar with psychosis) involves loss of contact with reality — hallucinations (hearing or seeing things), delusions (false fixed beliefs), disorganised thinking and behaviour. This is one of the most frightening presentations for families. Critically: the person is not "possessed," not dangerous by default, and is suffering deeply. Early treatment significantly improves outcomes.
સાઇકોસિસમાં વ્યક્તિ વાસ્તવિકતા સાથેનો સંપર્ક ગુમાવે છે — ભ્રમ, ભ્રામક વિચારો. આ ભૂત-પ્રેત નથી. આ એક ગંભીર તબીબી સ્થિતિ છે. વ્યક્તિ ખૂબ જ તકલીફ અનુભવે છે. વહેલું ઉપચાર ઘણો ફરક પાડે છે.
✓ Helpful
✓ Stay calm and consistent
✓ Do not argue with delusions
✓ Ensure medication is given
✓ Reduce stimulation at home
✗ Harmful
✗ Arguing with the beliefs
✗ Religious "treatments" instead of medicine
✗ Expressing frustration openly
✗ Leaving them unsupervised if unsafe
Addiction is a brain disease — not a moral failure or lack of willpower. Repeated substance use changes the brain's reward circuitry, making stopping genuinely difficult. In Gujarat (a dry state), alcohol use is common but hidden — this adds shame and delays help-seeking. Families often swing between enabling (giving money, covering up) and punishing (anger, threats). Both extremes worsen the illness.
વ્યસન એ મગજની બીમારી છે — નૈતિક નિષ્ફળતા નહીં. ગુજરાત દારૂ-નિષેધ રાજ્ય હોવા છતાં, ઉપયોગ સામાન્ય છે — આ શરમ ઉમેરે છે. પરિવાર ઘણી વખત "સુવિધા આપવા" (પૈસા, ઢાંકવું) અને "સજા" (ક્રોધ, ધમકી) વચ્ચે ઝૂલે છે. બંને ઉગ્રતા બીમારીને વધારે છે.
The family's role in recovery
Do not enable: do not give money for substances, cover up consequences, or lie to others about the problem.
Do not punish: threats, ultimatums, and anger rarely work and often delay recovery.
Do support: accompany to treatment, express concern calmly ("I am worried about you"), celebrate small wins.
Attend a support group — Al-Anon meetings exist in most districts for family members.

Understanding and breaking stigma
કલંક સમજો અને તોડો
Stigma — social shame and discrimination against people with mental illness — is one of the biggest barriers to care in India. It causes people to hide symptoms for years, avoid treatment, discontinue medication to "appear normal," and suffer in silence. Stigma lives in families too — fear of "what people will say," concern about children's marriage prospects, and shame about the diagnosis.
કલંક — માનસિક બીમારી ધરાવતા લોકો સામે સામાજિક શરમ — ભારતમાં ઉપચારનો સૌથી મોટો અવરોધ છે. "લોકો શું કહેશે" — આ ડરથી વ્યક્તિ વર્ષો સુધી લક્ષણ છુપાવે છે, ઉપચાર ટાળે છે.
How families can fight stigma
Use person-first language: "a person with depression" not "a depressed person" or "pagal."
Treat psychiatric appointments like any other medical appointment — no secrecy needed.
Educate extended family members calmly, with facts, not defensiveness.
Avoid discussing the person's illness publicly in ways that could humiliate them.
About marriage concerns: Many families delay or avoid treatment for fear it will affect the person's marriage prospects. In reality, untreated mental illness is a far greater obstacle to a stable marriage than a well-managed condition. A person who is stable on treatment, working, and functional is in a better position to marry than one who is unwell and untreated.
લગ્ન અંગે: ઘણા પરિવારો ઉપચાર મોડો પાડે છે કારણ કે તેઓ ડરે છે કે તે લગ્નની તકો ઘટાડશે. વાસ્તવમાં, સારી રીતે નિયંત્રિત સ્થિતિ ધરાવતી વ્યક્તિ અણઉપચારિત વ્યક્તિ કરતાં ઘણી સારી સ્થિતિમાં છે.

Joint family dynamics and mental illness
સંયુક્ત પરિવાર અને માનસિક બીમારી
The Indian joint family is both a remarkable resource — shared caregiving, financial support, constant companionship — and a potential source of stress — conflicting advice, criticism, invasion of privacy, and high expressed emotion (EE). Expressed emotion (EE) is a research term for the level of criticism, hostility, and emotional over-involvement in the home. High EE is one of the strongest predictors of relapse in schizophrenia and depression.
ભારતીય સંયુક્ત પરિવાર સંભાળ અને ટેકો માટે અદ્ભુત સ્ત્રોત છે — પણ ટીકા, વ્યક્તિગતતાનો અભાવ, અને ઉચ્ચ "expressed emotion" (EE) — ઘરમાં ટીકા, વૈરભાવ, ભાવનાત્મક અતિ-સંડોવણ — ફરી બીમારીનું મજબૂત આગાહી-સૂચક છે.
✓ Supportive family environment
✓ Consistent, calm daily routine
✓ One designated family contact for the doctor
✓ Allow privacy and space when needed
✓ Reduce arguments and household conflict
✓ Include the person in normal family activities
✓ Speak about them with respect in their absence
✗ High EE behaviours to avoid
✗ Frequent criticism of behaviour or appearance
✗ Discussing illness in front of extended family
✗ Overprotecting / doing everything for them
✗ Arguing about medication or treatment
✗ Comparing to siblings or others
✗ Pressuring recovery timeline
Who speaks to the doctor? In joint families, multiple members often want to attend appointments and give their version of events. This can overwhelm the patient and fragment care. We recommend designating one or two primary caregivers who attend appointments and communicate with the team. Others can be updated by them.
ડૉક્ટર સાથે કોણ વાત કરે? અમે ભલામણ કરીએ છીએ કે એક કે બે પ્રાથમિક સંભાળ આપનારા નિષ્ઠ ટૂટ નિમાય, જે ઓ.પી.ડી.માં આવે અને ટીમ સાથે વાતચીત કરે.

Caregiver fatigue and self-care
સંભાળ આપનારનો થાક અને આત્મ-સંભાળ
Caregiving for a family member with mental illness is one of the most demanding roles a person can take on. Caregiver burnout — physical, emotional, and mental exhaustion from sustained caregiving — is extremely common and often invisible. In India, the caregiver is frequently a woman — wife, mother, or daughter-in-law — who receives little recognition or support for her own wellbeing.
માનસિક બીમારી ધરાવતા સ્વજન માટે સંભાળ આપવી એ સૌથી કઠોર ભૂમિકાઓ પૈકીની એક છે. ભારતમાં, સંભાળ આપનાર ઘણી વખત સ્ત્રી — પત્ની, માતા, વહુ — હોય છે, જેને પોતાની સ્વસ્થતા માટે ઓછો ટેકો મળે છે.
Warning signs of caregiver burnout
Constant exhaustion
Resentment toward patient
Social withdrawal
Neglecting own health
Feeling trapped
Crying frequently
Anger / irritability
Feeling hopeless
Self-care is not selfish — it is essential
You cannot pour from an empty vessel. Your own health directly affects your ability to care.
Schedule at least 30 minutes daily for yourself — a walk, rest, prayer, or time with a friend.
Share the caregiving role with other family members — no one person should carry it alone.
Speak to Dr. Mehta or the counsellor at Aatmiya — caregiver wellbeing is part of treatment.
It is okay to feel angry, sad, or frustrated. These are normal responses to a very difficult situation.
💬
Communicating with your family member
પરિવારના સભ્ય સાથે સંવાદ
How you talk to and about your family member with mental illness has a direct biological effect on their stress response and recovery. Research consistently shows that tone, word choice, and frequency of criticism or warmth predict relapse and recovery more reliably than many clinical measures.
તમે સ્વજન સાથે કેવી રીતે વાત કરો છો તેની તેમના તણાવ-પ્રતિભાવ અને સ્વસ્થ થવા પર સીધી જૈવિક અસર છે.
Try these phrases instead
Instead of:
"Why are you like this? Pull yourself together."
Try:
"I can see you're struggling. I'm here with you."
Instead of:
"You don't need tablets. Just pray more / think positive."
Try:
"The doctor has prescribed these for a reason. Let's make sure you take them."
Instead of:
"You're ruining this family. Look what you're doing to us."
Try:
"I know this is hard for all of us. We will get through it together."

Recognising early warning signs of relapse
ફરી બીમારીના પ્રારંભિક ચેતવણી ચિહ્નો
Most relapses do not happen suddenly. There are usually 2–4 weeks of early warning signs that the family notices before the person themselves — or before the treating doctor sees them. Learning to recognise these signs and acting early can prevent a full relapse and hospitalisation.
મોટા ભાગની ફરી-બીમારી અચાનક આવતી નથી. સામાન્ય રીતે ૨–૪ અઠવાડિયા પહેલા ચેતવણી ચિહ્નો આવે છે. આ ઓળખવાથી સંપૂર્ણ ફરી-બીમારી ટાળી શકાય છે.
Common early warning signs across conditions
Sleep changes
Social withdrawal
Stopping medication
Appetite changes
Increased irritability
Renewed substance use
Talking to self
Neglecting hygiene
Missing work / duties
Unusual energy / elation
Religious preoccupation
Spending unusually
What to do when you notice warning signs
Do not wait for the next appointment. Call Aatmiya Hospital and describe what you're seeing.
Ensure medication is being taken — many relapses are triggered by stopping tablets.
Reduce stress in the home environment — postpone conflicts, avoid arguments.
Accompany to an urgent appointment even if the person resists — early intervention saves months of recovery.
🆘 Emergency signs — act immediately
Talking about suicide or self-harm · Threatening to harm others · Completely unable to care for self · Not recognising family members · Aggressive behaviour that poses risk · Refusing all food and water for more than 24 hours.
Call Aatmiya Hospital immediately. Emergency: 108. Police: 100.

Common myths and the facts
સામાન્ય ગેરસમજ અને સત્ય
❌ Myth
"Mental illness is caused by black magic or evil spirits."
✓ Fact
Mental illness is caused by brain chemistry, genetics, stress, and life experiences. It has biological, psychological, and social causes — no supernatural ones.
❌ Myth
"Psychiatric medicines are addictive and dangerous. Better to avoid."
✓ Fact
Most psychiatric medications (antidepressants, antipsychotics, mood stabilisers) are not addictive. They are essential treatment. Stopping abruptly is dangerous — always consult the doctor first.
❌ Myth
"People with mental illness are dangerous and violent."
✓ Fact
People with mental illness are far more likely to be victims of violence than perpetrators. Most are not dangerous. Untreated illness and substance use together increase risk — both can be treated.
❌ Myth
"Once mentally ill, always mentally ill. No cure."
✓ Fact
Many people achieve full recovery. Even those with chronic conditions can live productive, fulfilling lives with proper treatment and support. Recovery is a journey, not a destination.
❌ Myth
"Visiting a psychiatrist means you are 'pagal' (mad)."
✓ Fact
A psychiatrist treats all mental and emotional disorders — from stress and sleep problems to anxiety, depression, and addiction. Seeing one means you are taking your health seriously — like seeing a cardiologist for the heart.
